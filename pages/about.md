## About FPJ

Fernando Poe Jr. was born Ronald Allan Kelley Poe in Manila on August 20, 1939, the son of actor Fernando Poe Sr. He entered the film industry as a stuntman after his father's death and made his screen debut in 1955 in *Anak ni Palaris*.

Over the next five decades he appeared in around 300 films, and became known simply as FPJ, or "Da King" of Philippine cinema. He also directed, often under the name Ronwaldo Reyes.

He won the FAMAS Award for Best Actor five times and entered the FAMAS Hall of Fame in 1988. He ran for president in 2004 and died in Quezon City on December 14 of that year. He was later named a National Artist of the Philippines for Film.

## Rationale

This project aims to collect and present the works of Fernando Poe Jr. We find his works relevant because they are among the clearest records of how Filipino popular cinema spoke to ordinary Filipinos. Across almost five decades and around 300 films, FPJ returned again and again to the same hero: a common man, often a driver, a farmer, a barangay chairman or a low-ranking policeman, who stands up to the powerful when the law fails the weak. In films such as *Kapag Puno Na ang Salop* and *Ako ang Huhusga*, the villain is a corrupt judge; in others it is a crooked businessman, a landlord or a syndicate. These stories put values that Filipino audiences hold dear, such as justice, loyalty, dignity, family and *pakikisama*, at the center of mass entertainment, and that is why the *masa* embraced him as their own.

His films are also worth studying as works of art and craft. FPJ was not only an actor but a director, writer and producer who shaped the look and rhythm of the Filipino action film, and his work was honored with FAMAS awards for both acting and directing and, after his death, the title of National Artist for Film. The collection also shows his range beyond action: romantic comedies with Susan Roces, family comedies, and fantasy and period films.

Finally, his influence is still alive today. *Ang Probinsyano* was remade into one of the longest-running television series in Philippine history, and his restored films continue to be shown on television and online. Many of these films, however, are scattered across different uploads, and some survive only in worn copies. By gathering them in one place, with basic information about each, we hope to make it easier for students and new audiences to watch, appreciate and remember the work of Da King.

## About this collection

This site gathers a selection of his films. Each entry has the full film to watch, embedded from YouTube, with its title, year of release, and a short note about it. You can [browse the films]({{ '/browse.html' | relative_url }}), follow them on the [timeline]({{ '/timeline.html' | relative_url }}), or sort and download the [film list]({{ '/data.html' | relative_url }}).
